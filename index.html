Understanding intercellular crosstalk for cognitive enhancement

“Can memory be enhanced by targeting neuron–astrocyte or body–brain crosstalk?”

Research Focus 01

Neuron–astrocyte crosstalk in learning and memory

  • Learning and memory depend on plastic changes in synaptic transmission.
  • Astrocytes contact synapses and exchange signals with neurons, shaping synaptic plasticity.
  • Modulating astrocyte function is a promising strategy for enhancing cognition.
  • The mechanisms of neuron–astrocyte communication during learning and memory remain unclear.

We combine molecular, cellular, electrophysiological, genetic, and behavioral approaches to discover new modes of neuron–astrocyte communication.

Our goal is to develop strategies that improve cognition in both healthy and diseased brains.

Research Focus 02

Body–brain crosstalk in learning and memory

Background & Hypothesis
  • Physical activity, including exercise, can improve memory.
  • Exercise-induced factors released from peripheral organs, especially skeletal muscle, contribute to these cognitive benefits.
  • Additional muscle-derived signals that influence brain function may remain undiscovered.
Key Findings & Strategy
  • Using organ-specific protein labeling, we identified multiple exercise-induced factors released from muscle.
  • Raising blood levels of selected muscle-derived factors reproduced the memory benefits of exercise.
  • We are investigating how these factors act on the brain to enhance memory.
Research Focus 03

Restoring cognitive function in the diseased adult brain

1

Why epilepsy?

  • One of the three most common neurological disorders
  • Often begins in adulthood
  • About 35% of cases remain drug-resistant
  • Frequently disrupts learning and memory
2

Synaptic pathology of Temporal Lobe Epilepsy (TLE)

  • Hyperconnected, hyperactive neural circuits
  • Abnormal synaptic structure and activity
  • Impaired synaptic plasticity and memory
  • Molecular mechanisms at synapses remain unclear
3

Our approach

  • Joint KBRI–DGIST–Sungkyunkwan–Ewha research program
  • Ultrasound stimulation paired with high-sensitivity neural activity monitoring
  • Preclinical and clinical evaluation of stimulation efficacy
  • Identify synaptic mechanisms that suppress seizures and restore cognition.
